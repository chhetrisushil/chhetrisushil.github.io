Current work

Loom — an execution kernel for agentic applications

Loom treats an agentic workflow as a process that unfolds over time. Every step and decision is recorded as an immutable fact in an append-only log, so state is fold(events) rather than a mutable blob you hope stayed in sync. From that one property everything else follows: an execution can be paused for a human, resumed on another machine, replayed bit-for-bit to debug it, forked to explore alternatives, and evaluated against its own history.

Flows are ordinary async TypeScript — no DSL, no YAML, no state machines. The kernel stays small and deliberately boring; capability lives in packages and plugins behind versioned SPIs with conformance kits, so a third-party backend wires up exactly like a first-party one.

  • Durability as the default, not a feature. The log is the source of truth. Replay re-folds it to identical state, and completed steps are served from the log so effects never run twice.
  • Human-in-the-loop as one line. ctx.suspend() writes a durable checkpoint and hands control back. The process can restart or move machines; when the approval arrives it resumes from exactly that point.
  • Decisions written down. Every trade-off is an ADR — including the ones that record a decision not to build something, with the evidence that was missing.
  • Guarantees that check themselves. Fitness functions in CI assert the ABI can't drift, stored logs stay foldable, and a capability's maturity badge can't claim evidence it lacks.

About

Nineteen years, mostly on the parts that have to keep working

I am an engineering manager at Google, based in Bengaluru. I studied information technology at the University of Mumbai, and I have spent about nineteen years building software — long enough to have watched several confident architectures meet production and lose.

The through-line in what I enjoy is systems whose correctness you can actually argue about: explicit contracts, recorded decisions, failure modes that are loud instead of silent. Loom is the current expression of that. Managing a team has not changed the interest so much as sharpened it — most of what makes software survivable is decided before anyone writes the clever part.

I speak English, Hindi and Nepali.
